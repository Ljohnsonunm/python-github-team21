# python-github-team
ABSTRACT:
Our project focuses on food insecurity and food access in Cuba, New Mexico. We want to understand whether residents have access to affordable, nutritious food and how local agriculture may affect food availability. We will use three datasets: The U.S. Census Bureau’s American Community Survey, the USDA Food Access Research Atlas, and the USDA Cropland Data Layer. The U.S. Census Bureau's dataset will help us examine income, poverty, SNAP participation, and vehicle availability. The Food Access Research Atlas will help us investigate access to food retailers and potential transportation barriers. The 2022 USDA Census of Agriculture will provide information about farmland, crop production, and agricultural activity in Sandoval County, helping us understand the regional agricultural context related to food insecurity in Cuba, New Mexico. These datasets are suitable because they provide information about economic conditions, food access, and land use. We expect to identify patterns that may be related to food insecurity and determine which factors deserve further attention. Our results could help increase the understanding of food-access challenges in the community and brainstorm possible improvements. Our team will collect and clean local data and county-level agricultural data, analyze it, and create visualizations. We will present our results through Streamlit and document our work on GitHub.

EXPECTED CONTRIBUTIONS: 
Our project will provide a clearer picture of how agriculture and food insecurity may be connected in Cuba, New Mexico. By bringing together information from different sources, we hope to highlight gaps in available data and identify questions that could benefit from further research. Our findings may help community members, local organizations, and future researchers better understand the challenges surrounding food access in the area. The project will also provide a starting point for future studies exploring ways to strengthen local food resources and improve access to nutritious food. Additionally, our completed project will serve as a reference for others interested in studying similar issues in rural New Mexico communities.
